# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

## Setup and running

Python 3.14 with **pygame-ce** (the community fork; upstream `pygame` 2.6.1 has no Python 3.14 wheels and fails to build). pygame-ce is a drop-in replacement — code still does `import pygame`. Never install both `pygame` and `pygame-ce` in the same venv; they share the `pygame` module name.

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
.venv/bin/python sheep.py
```

Headless smoke test (no window; posts QUIT after 3 s):

```bash
SDL_VIDEODRIVER=dummy .venv/bin/python -c "
import pygame, sheep
from threading import Timer
Timer(3, lambda: pygame.event.post(pygame.event.Event(pygame.QUIT))).start()
sheep.run()"
```

There are no tests or linter configured.

## Origin

`sheep.py` started as a copy of `../boids/boids.py` (bird flocking) and keeps its structure: module-level tunables, `Grid` neighbour lookup, steering forces summed in `acceleration()`, snapshot update (now in `World.step()`). Lessons recorded in `../boids/CLAUDE.md` mostly still apply.

## Architecture

Single file, `sheep.py`. All tunables are module-level constants at the top. Units are pixels/second for speeds and pixels/second² for forces; the simulation steps with a fixed `DT = 1/FPS`. Top-down view of a fenced pasture with a pen in the bottom-right corner; the goal is to get the whole herd into the pen.

`World` holds everything: `herd` (sheep and lambs), `bales`, `dog`, `grass`, the gate and the pen `walls`, the clock and `darkness`. `World.step()` advances one step and `World.draw()` renders it; `run()` only handles input. Sheep and the dog take the world (`acceleration(world)`, `move(accel, world)`, `Dog.update(world)`), so a headless test is: `w = World()`, `w.click_hay(pos)`, `w.click_dog(pos)`, then call `w.step()` in a loop. `R` replaces the world with a new one.

Controls: left click places a hay bale (`Bale`) or removes one you click on; bales shrink as they're eaten and disappear when empty. Right click lets the sheepdog (`Dog`) into the field, or moves it; right-clicking the dog removes it. G opens and closes the gate, H toggles bars over the sheep showing how full each is (1 − hunger: a full green bar = not hungry), R restarts, Esc quits. `draw_status()` shows the clock, herd counts, grass left, penned count, the time the whole herd took to be penned (`World.penned_at`), and the controls.

How sheep differ from the boids:

- **Sheep can stand still.** There is no `MIN_SPEED`, so facing (`Sheep.heading`) is stored separately from `vel`. It turns towards the velocity only above `FACE_SPEED`, ramping to the full `TURN_RATE` at `FACE_FULL_SPEED`. Slower nudges from neighbours are side-steps that don't turn the body; a low threshold made grazing sheep in tight groups spin on the spot. Grazing sheep instead turn slowly by `wander * GRAZE_TURN`.
- **Wander scales with goal speed.** At full strength on a grazing sheep, the sideways push (20 px/s²) outran `GRAZE_SPEED`, the body turned to follow it, and the next push was sideways again, so sheep walked in circles (~105°/s turning, up to 8 spins per 40 s).
- **Goal-speed term instead of cruise thrust.** After the force limit, `(heading * goal - vel) * SPEED_GAIN` pulls the *whole* velocity towards the goal (friction, not momentum). Goal = `GRAZE_SPEED` or `WALK_SPEED`, blended towards `RUN_SPEED` by alarm. This is the speed source that keeps the snapshot update from stalling.
- **Grazing/walking states.** Each sheep switches at random (mean `GRAZE_TIME` / `WALK_TIME`). `FOLLOW` makes a grazing sheep likelier to set off when its neighbours walk. At `FOLLOW = 10` walking spread through the herd (~50% walking) and it broke into ~40 groups; at 4 it's ~25% walking.
- **Alarm, 0..1.** Set by `avoid_dog()`, decays over `ALARM_TIME`, and spreads: each sheep takes at least `ALARM_SPREAD` × its most alarmed neighbour's alarm. Alarm raises speed, speed cap (`PANIC_SPEED`), force limit (`PANIC_FORCE`, so running sheep can still turn) and cohesion (`ALARM_COHESION`, the "selfish herd" bunching).
- **State changes are deferred.** `acceleration()` writes `_next_walking`/`_next_alarm` and `move()` applies them, so every sheep reacts to the same snapshot. `avoid_dog()` and the `seek_*()` methods override those after `update_state()`. `acceleration()` also sets `_eating` (bale) and `_grazing` (grass cell), which `feed()`, called from `move()`, uses to update hunger, bales and grass.
- **What a calm sheep does, in order** (`acceleration()`): a lamb too far from its mother goes after her (`follow_mother()`); at night it rests; otherwise hay (`seek_food()`), then grass (`seek_grass()`), then company if it's alone (`seek_company()`). A queueing sheep (stopped at a bale, `_face` set, not eating) nibbles the grass underfoot if hungry enough. Without that, ~35 sheep starved in hay queues while standing on grass, and the herd's wool dropped to 0.87; with it, ~6 starve and wool stays ~0.94.
- Alignment matches neighbours' mean *velocity* (`(mean_vel - vel) * gain`), not "fly at max speed their way", because calm sheep move slowly or not at all. Cohesion is a spring towards neighbours' centre, scaled by `WALK_COHESION` for walking sheep. At full strength, a sheep walking off was pulled back into a U-turn through its group. Lower values give straighter walks but split the herd (0.1 → ~21 groups); 0.5 is the compromise.
- **The fence is solid.** `edges()` turns sheep back near the fence, and `move()` also clamps position inside `_FIELD_MIN.._FIELD_MAX_*`, zeroing the velocity into the fence.
- **Bales are finite.** `Bale.hay` (0..1) drops by `DT / BALE_HAY` per eating sheep per step. `Bale.radius` shrinks with √hay down to `HAY_MIN_RADIUS`, and `Bale.reach` (eating distance) follows it: radius + `SHEEP_RADIUS` + `HAY_MARGIN` + `EAT_GAP`. Reach must lie beyond the `avoid_hay()` push zone; with a 10 px zone and a fixed 40 px reach, only 4–7 sheep could eat. `Sheep._eating` is the `Bale` being eaten (or `None`), so `move()` knows which bale to deplete. Herd demand is ~16 sheep eating at once (140 × `HUNGER_RATE` / `EAT_RATE`), so a 900 sheep-second bale lasts ~1–4 minutes depending on how busy it is.
- Hay: sheep walk to the nearest bale within `FOOD_RADIUS × (1 + HUNGER_SIGHT × hunger)`, so starving sheep look twice as far. Near a bale, a hungry sheep heads for the nearest free spot among the `RING_SPOTS` eating places round it (`Bale.free_spots()`, computed once per step: cached against the current `Grid`, which is rebuilt every step), walking round to the far side if needed. Before that, sheep queued behind the first eater in the way: only 3–4 ate per bale, all on the side facing the herd, while ~25 starved in the queue. With free spots, up to ~8 eat per bale and starving sheep roughly halved. Only when no spot is free does the queueing rule apply: a sheep with a slow neighbour (`BLOCKED_SPEED`) close ahead of it towards the bale (`QUEUE_COS`) stops and grazes where it is. Without that queueing, the crowd behind kept pushing and piled sheep on top of each other. Sheep stopped at a bale, eating or queueing, record it in `_face` (set in `seek_food()`, used in `move()`). They turn to face it at `HAY_TURN`, swaying ±`HAY_SWAY` with their wander, instead of the grazing drift; this is skipped when alarmed. Without it, ~80% faced away. Healthy now: ~85–95% within 45° of the bale.
- **Bales are solid.** `avoid_hay()` pushes sheep away within `HAY_MARGIN` of touching a bale; this is added after the limit, like `edges()`. `move()` then clamps every sheep to at least `HAY_RADIUS + SHEEP_RADIUS` from each bale's centre and removes inward velocity, like the fence. `SHEEP_RADIUS` is 13 because the rump and side wool reach that far; at 10 the wool overlapped the bale. The head (~19 px forward) still reaches over the bale's edge to eat. `move()` takes the bale list for this.

- **Hunger, 0..1.** Random at start. It rises at `HUNGER_RATE` and falls at `EAT_RATE` while eating. At 0 the sheep is `full`: it ignores hay, and if it's within `LEAVE_DISTANCE` of a bale it turns its back (via `_face`) and walks off, freeing its place for the queue. It becomes hungry again at `HUNGRY_AT`; without that gap, a sheep wants hay again a moment after leaving. One eating sheep offsets ~9 grazing ones (`EAT_RATE / HUNGER_RATE`), so with 140 sheep it takes ~16 eating at once (two bales or more) for hunger to stop climbing.
- **Grass** (`Grass`): the field is a grid of `GRASS_CELL` squares with 0..1 grass each. Sheep prefer hay: they only call `seek_grass()` when no hay is in sight. They start grazing at `GRASS_HUNGER` and stop at `GRASS_SATED` (hysteresis, like `full` for hay). A grazing sheep loses hunger at `GRASS_EAT_RATE`, a third of hay's, and eats `GRASS_BITE`/s from its cell. On a bare cell (below `GRASS_MIN`) it samples cells at `GRASS_LOOK` distances in 8 directions and walks to the greenest one on its side of the pen fence (`_grass_target`, kept until reached or no longer better). That's what moves the herd round the field. Worn cells regrow at `GRASS_REGROW`, updated every `GRASS_TICK`; only cells in `worn` are visited. The look is a one-pixel-per-cell `wear` surface of dirt-brown alpha, smooth-scaled to the window and redrawn at most every 0.25 s (only `dirty` cells change).
- **Starving** (hunger ≥ `STARVING`): bleats ("Baa!" bubbles via `say()`, `bubble()` caches the rendered surface), speed scaled down by `strength()`, and `wool` drops to `WOOL_MIN`. Wool regrows when hunger is below `WOOL_REGROW_BELOW`. `draw()` shrinks and greys the fleece with low wool.
- **Lambs**: `Sheep(pos, mother)` with `size = LAMB_SIZE`; `radius` (for walls and bales) and drawing scale with size. `follow_mother()` trots (`LAMB_TROT`) after the mother when she's more than `LAMB_STAY` away, until it's back within `LAMB_CLOSE`, and it bleats "Maa!" beyond `LAMB_LOST`. Healthy: median lamb–mother distance ~28 px, 90% under ~55.
- **Day and night**: `World.hour` runs from `START_HOUR` through a `DAY_LENGTH`-second day; `darkness_at()` is 0 by day, 1 from `NIGHT_START` to `NIGHT_END`, smooth over `DUSK_HOURS`. Darkness strengthens cohesion (`NIGHT_COHESION`) and the lonely search (`DUSK_LONELY`), so the herd gathers. Above `REST_DARK`, sheep that aren't too hungry (`NIGHT_HUNGER`) or alarmed rest: goal speed 0, hunger at `NIGHT_HUNGER_RATE`, and `rest_pose` eases them into the lying pose in `draw()`. The dog sleeps too. `World.draw()` tints the scene: warm at dusk and dawn, blue at night.
- **Pen and gate**: `pen_walls()` gives the pen's two inner sides as segments, plus the gate segment when shut; the field fence makes the other two sides. Walls are solid: `push_out_of_walls()` in `move()` (and for the dog), plus a soft `avoid_walls()` push. `in_pen()` decides which side something is on; `Sheep.inside` is refreshed in `move()`. Neighbours, the dog and grass targets on the other side of the fence are ignored, or the herd would press the penned sheep against it. `route()` sends anything whose target is on the other side through the gate via the `_GATE_OUT`/`_GATE_IN` waypoints, or returns `None` when the gate is shut. Every seek (hay, free spots, mother, company, the dog's target) goes through it. `Bale.free_spots()` drops spots on the wrong side of, or too close to, a wall. The dog's `pick_target()` keeps out of the pen unless it's shut inside. Hay in the pen lures in the sheep that can see it (~50 of 164 in 2 minutes); the rest need the dog.
- **Lonely sheep rejoin.** A sheep that sees no neighbours, isn't going for hay and isn't at a bale calls `seek_company()`. If no sheep is within `VIEW_RADIUS` in any direction, it walks to the nearest one within `LONELY_RADIUS`, using `Grid.nearby(pos, cells)` with a wider block. Over 4 minutes with the dog loose, this cut isolated sheep from ~5 to ~0.5 and groups from ~18 to ~14.
- **The dog is autonomous and familiar.** `Dog.update()` wanders between waypoints: `pick_target()` chooses a spot well inside the fence, `DOG_TRIP` away. The dog walks there at `DOG_WALK_SPEED`, turning at most `DOG_TURN` and weaving ±`DOG_WEAVE` with its OU wander. On arrival it sometimes (`DOG_SNIFF_CHANCE`) stands and sniffs for a few seconds. Every ~`DOG_SPRINT_EVERY` s it sprints for 1–2.5 s in a random direction, then picks a new target. The earlier version turned at `wander * rate` with no destination, which made tight circles (~25 px wide) whenever the wander stayed one way: 15 full loops per 50 fifteen-second windows, path only 41% straight. Now 0 loops and ~86% straight over 10 s. It swings back towards the middle near the fence (only reachable by sprinting) and goes round bales. `Dog.sprint` (0..1, from its current speed) scales how sheep react: the avoid radius (`DOG_AVOID_RADIUS`) and alarm (`DOG_ALARM`, both walk/sprint pairs). Sheep step aside from a walking dog almost calmly; a sprint makes them hurry, but alarm stays below ~0.45, so there's no stampede.

Unchanged from boids: `Grid` cells are `VIEW_RADIUS` wide (grow them if perception ever reaches further), `avoid_dog()` must fade to zero with distance, wander is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck walk, and `limit()` caps the flocking forces before the goal-speed, edge, hay and dog terms are added. 164 animals cost ~4 ms/step with bales, grass and the dog.

## Verifying behaviour

Judge from metrics over time, stepping a `World` headless (`World.step()`; `World.draw()` onto the dummy display for screenshots): mean speed, walking count, mean alarm, nearest-neighbour distance, number of groups (single-linkage at 60 px). Healthy values for the current tuning with no mouse input: mean speed ~8–12, ~30% walking, nearest neighbour ~25–30 px, ~6–12 groups. For spinning, track heading change per step: grazing sheep ~22°/s, walking sheep ~45–55°/s, and under ~3% of walking bouts should turn more than 270° in total (loops). Average over a few seeds; single runs vary a lot. With the dog loose: max alarm ~0.05 while it walks and ≤ ~0.45 during sprints, top sheep speed ~100–120, and the dog stays inside the fence. For a long run, 10 game minutes (36,000 steps, ~70 s of wall time) with 5 bales and the dog: no sheep closer to a pen wall than its radius, ~150 of 164 resting in the middle of the night, grazing taking over (60–90 sheep) once the hay is gone, and starving sheep in single figures after the first minute. With two bales: ~8–16 eating at once, full sheep almost never within `Bale.reach` of a bale, and mean hunger levelling off around 0.5–0.7. To check the look, draw each sheep's path over ~10 s as lines ("long exposure"): walks should be gentle curves, not curls. Render frames with `draw()` onto a surface under `SDL_VIDEODRIVER=dummy` and save them with `pygame.image.save`.
